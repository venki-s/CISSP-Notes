## 1. Apply Security Principles to Site and Facility Design.  

## 1.1 Secure Facility Plan. 
1. Secure facility plan - Outlines the security needs of your organization and emphasizes methods or mechanisms to employ to provide security. 
2. Critical path analysis - Identifying relationships between mission-critical applications, processes, and operations.  
3. Technology convergence - The tendency for various technologies, solutions, utilities, and systems to evolve and merge over time. 

## 1.2 Site Selection.  
Visibility - Where are the closest emergency services located? Are there unique hazards? Locations of security cameras.
Natural disasters.  
Facilities design - Crime Prevention through Environmental Design (CPTED).  
  1. Four principles: access control, natural surveillance, image and milieu(visual elements and aesthetics of an environment and Milieu encompasses the broader environment), and territorial control (attempt to make the area feel like an inclusive, caring community. ).
  2. Second-generation CPTED has four principles: social cohesion, community culture, connectivity, and threshold capacity(Threshold capacity refers to the ability of a community or neighborhood to absorb and respond to external influences while maintaining its stability and security.).

## 1.3 Implement Site and Facility Security Controls. 

1. Administrative physical security controls - Include facility construction and selection, personnel controls, awareness training, emergency response. 
2. Technical physical security controls - Include access controls, intrusion detection, alarms, monitoring, heating, ventilation, HVAC, power supplies, fire detection and suppression. 
3. Physical physical security controls - Includes fences, lighting, locks, mantraps, dogs, guards.  
4. Functional order controls should be used:   
   a. Deterrence - Boundary restrictions. 
   b. Denial - direct access to physical assets should be denied. 
   c. Detect - your system needs to detect intrusion. 
   d. Delay - If the breach is successful, then the intruder should be delayed sufficiently in their access attempts. 
   e. Determine - Security staff or legal authorities should determine the cause of the incident.  
   f. Decide - Decide on the response to implement.  

## 1.3 Equipment Failure. 
1. Equipment failure is a common cause of a loss of availability. 
2. When deciding on strategies to maintain availability, it is often important to understand 
   a. Allowable interruption window (AIW), service delivery objective (SDO), and maximum tolerable downtime/outage (MTD/MTO).  
3. The schedule for such operations should be based on the mean time to failure (MTTF) and mean time to repair (MTTR) 
4. An additional measurement is that of the mean time between failures (MTBF).

## 1.4 Wiring Closets.  
1. A cable plant is the collection of interconnected cables and intermediary devices (such as cross-connects, patch panels, and switches) that establish the physical network. Elements of a cable plant. 
  a. An element of cable plant management policy. 
  b. Entrance facility - aka demarcation point, where the cable enters the building. 
  c. Equipment room - Main wiring closet. 
  d. Backbone distribution system - Between equipment room and telecommunication rooms. 
  e. Telecommunications room - Serves the connection needs of a floor or a section of a large building. 
  f. Horizontal distribution system - Between the telecommunication room and work areas. 

## 1.5 Server Rooms/ Data Center.   
1. A server room is often not optimized for workers but for housing equipment. Data centers can include gas-based halon-substitute oxygen-displacement fire detection and extinguishing systems, walls with a one-hour minimum fire rating, low temperatures, and little or no lighting. 
2. Server rooms should be located at the core of the building. Avoid locating the data center on the ground floor, top floor, and basement whenever possible. The server room should also be located away from water, gas, and sewage lines.  
3. A data center could be a single-tenant configuration or a multitenant configuration. 
4. Smartcards - Credit-card-sized, IDs, badges, security passes with an embedded magnetic strip, bar code, or integrated circuit chip
5. Memory cards - Machine-readable ID cards with a magnetic strip. 
6. Proximity reader - A proximity device can be a passive device, a field-powered device, or a transponder. When it passes near a proximity reader, the reader device is able to determine who the bearer is and whether they have authorized access.
7. Intrusion detection system - Systems designed to detect a breach or attack
8. Motion Detection
   a. A digital motion detector monitors for significant or meaningful changes in the digital pattern of a monitored area. This is effectively a smart security camera. 
   b. A passive infrared (PIR) or heat-based motion detector monitors for significant or meaningful changes in a monitored area's heat levels. 
   c. A wave pattern motion detector or microwave motion detector transmits a consistent low ultrasonic or high microwave frequency signal into a monitored area and monitors for significant or meaningful changes or disturbances in the reflected pattern. 
   d. A capacitance motion detector senses changes in the electrical or magnetic field surrounding a monitored object. 
   e. A photoelectric motion detector senses changes in visible light levels for the monitored area. Photoelectric motion detectors are usually deployed in internal rooms with no windows and are kept dark.   
   g. A passive audio motion detector listens for abnormal sounds in the monitored area. 
9. Intrusion Alarms
   a. Deterrent alarms: Alarms that trigger deterrents may engage additional locks, shut doors, and so on.  
   b. Repellent alarms: Alarms that trigger repellents usually sound an audio siren or bell and turn on lights. 
   c. Notification alarms: Alarms that trigger notification are often silent from the intruder/attacker perspective but record data about the incident and notify administrators, security guards, and law enforcement.
   Alarms are also categorized by location: local, centralized, or auxiliary.
   a. Local alarm system: Local alarm systems must broadcast an audible alarm signal that can be easily heard from a distance. 
   b. Central station system: The alarm is usually silent locally, but off-site monitoring agents are notified to respond to the security breach. 
   c. Auxiliary alarm system: Auxiliary alarm systems can be added to either local or centralized alarm systems.  

## 1.6 Utilities and HVAC Considerations. 
1. The first stage or level of power management is using surge protectors. The next level is to use a power conditioner or power-line conditioner. The third level of power protection is to use an uninterruptible power supply (UPS). 
2. There are two main types of UPSs: double conversion and line interactive. A double conversion UPS functions by taking power in from the wall outlet, storing it in a battery, pulling power out of it. A line-interactive UPS has a surge protector, battery charger/inverter, and voltage regulator positioned between the grid power source and the equipment. 
3. Power Issues 
   a. Fault: Momentary complete loss of power. 
   b. Blackout: Prolonged complete loss of power. 
   c. Sag: Momentary low voltage. 
   d. Brownout: Prolonged low voltage. 
   e. Spike: Momentary high voltage. 
   f. Surge: Prolonged high voltage. 
   g. Inrush: An initial spike of power usually associated with connecting to a power source. 
   h. Ground: The wire in an electrical circuit that provides an alternate pathway for electricity to flow safely to the earth (i.e., the ground). 
4. Noise - A steady interfering power disturbance or fluctuation. 
5. EMI - electromagnetic interference has two types. 
   a. Common mode noise is generated by a difference in power between the hot and ground wires. 
   b. Traverse mode noise is generated by a difference in power between the hot and neutral wires. 
6. Radio frequency interference - RFI, electrical appliances generate RFI like fluorescent lights. 
7. Transient - A short duration of line noise.  
8. Clean - Nonfluctuating pure power. 
9. Ground - The wire in an electrical circuit that is grounded.  

## 1.7 Temperature, Humidity, and Static
1. Humidity in a computer room should be maintained between 40-60%
2. 1500 volts causes destruction of data stored on hard drives
3. We also recommend that you maintain positive air pressure in the data center as well as superior levels of air filtration.
4. Additionally, humidity (i.e., relative humidity [RH]) in a computer room should be maintained between 20 and 80 percent.

## 1.8 Fire

1. In fire triangle The three corners of the triangle represent fuel, heat, and oxygen. The center of the triangle represents the chemical reaction among these three elements. The purpose of the fire triangle is to illustrate that if you can remove any one of the four items from the fire triangle, the fire can be extinguished. Different suppression mediums address different aspects of the fire:
2. Water suppresses the temperature.
3. Soda acid and other dry powders suppress the fuel supply.
4. Aqueous film forming foam (AFFF) suppresses temperature and fuel supply.
5. Carbon dioxide (CO2)  suppresses the oxygen supply.
6. Halon substitutes and other nonflammable gases interfere with the chemistry of combustion and/or suppress the oxygen supply.

### 1.8.1 The four stages of fire.  

Stage 1: The Incipient Stage At this stage, there is only air ionization and no smoke.  
Stage 2: The Smoke Stage In Stage 2, smoke is visible from the point of ignition.  
Stage 3: The Flame Stage This is when a flame can be seen with the naked eye. 
Stage 4: The Heat Stage At Stage 4, the fire is considerably further down the timescale to the point where there is an intense heat buildup and everything in the area burns.

### 1.8.2 Fire detection systems.  
Fixed temperature detection - Triggers suppression when a specific temperature is reached. 
Rate of rise detection - Triggers detection if a temperature increases at a specific speed.  
Flame actuated system - Triggers suppression based on infrared energy of flames.   
Smoke actuated system - Uses photoelectric or radioactive ionization sensors.  

### 1.8.3 Water supression system
1. A wet pipe system (also known as a closed head system) is always full of water. Water discharges immediately when suppression is triggered. 
2. A dry pipe system contains a compressed inert gas. Once suppression is triggered, the inert gas is released.  
3. A preaction system is a variation of the dry pipe system that uses a two-stage detection and release mechanism. The system exists as a dry pipe until the initial stages of a fire (smoke, heat, and so on) are detected, and then the pipes are allowed to fill with water (Stage 1).

### Implement and Manage Physical Security
1. Fences 3-4 feet high deter casual trespassers. 
2. Fences 6-7 feet high discourage most intruders except determined ones.  
3. Fences 8 feet or more high with three strands of barbed wire deter even determined intruders. 
4. Gates - Controlled exit and entry points in a fence.  
5. Turnstile - Restricts movement in one direction.  
6. Mantrap - A double set of doors that is often protected by a guard.  
7. Perimeter protection needs lights with 2 foot-candles of power. 
8. If a lighted area is 40 feet in diameter, poles should be 40 feet apart. 5 feet apart in parkades
9. You need to be able to handle visitors
10. Lock - A crude form of identification and authorization mechanism
Electronic access control lock - Has an electromagnet to keep the door locked, a credential reader for authentication, and a sensor to reengage the electromagnet when the door is closed
Local alarm systems - Must broadcast an audible alarm heard up to 400 feet away (up to 120 decibel)
Central station system - Usually silent locally, but offsite monitoring agents are notified
Auxiliary station - Emergency services are notified
Closed circuit TV is not an automated detection and response system, it needs people watching it
Human safety is always the most important factor
Privacy means protecting personal information from discloser - NIST 800-122, GDPR
You are usually obliged to have physical security controls
