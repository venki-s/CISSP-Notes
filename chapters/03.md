
# CISSP Chapter 3 — Business Continuity Planning

**Domains:**
- 1.7 — Identify, analyze, assess, prioritize, and implement Business Continuity requirements
- 7.13 — Participate in Business Continuity planning and exercises

---

## 1. Business Continuity Planning (BCP)

**BCP:** A strategic process for maintaining critical business operations during disruptions, even with reduced resources or infrastructure.

**Primary objectives:**
- Protect human life and safety.
- Maintain mission-critical business functions.
- Minimize operational and financial disruption.
- Enable timely recovery of normal operations.

### BCP vs. DRP

| BCP | DRP |
|---|---|
| Business Continuity Planning | Disaster Recovery Planning |
| Strategic, business-oriented | Tactical, technical-oriented |
| Maintains critical business processes | Restores disrupted systems and infrastructure |
| Focuses on continuity of operations | Focuses on recovery mechanisms |
| Examples: alternative business processes, staffing arrangements | Examples: backups, recovery sites, fault tolerance |

**Exam point:** BCP and DRP overlap. The distinction is primarily their perspective, not a rigid sequence.

**Highest priority in both BCP and DRP: Human safety, before IT recovery.**

---

## 2. Four Main Phases of BCP

1. **Project Scope and Planning**
2. **Business Impact Analysis (BIA)**
3. **Continuity Planning**
4. **Plan Approval and Implementation**

### Phase 1 — Project Scope and Planning

Four activities:

1. **Organizational Review:** Identify critical departments, business functions, stakeholders, facilities, and locations.
2. **BCP Team Selection:** Assemble a cross-functional team.
3. **Resource Requirements:** Identify personnel, time, funding, and infrastructure requirements.
4. **External Dependencies:** Identify third-party, contractual, legal, and regulatory dependencies.

### BCP Team Composition

Include representatives from:
- Business and operational departments
- IT and cybersecurity
- Physical security and facilities
- Legal and compliance
- Human resources
- Public relations and communications
- Senior management

**Senior management responsibilities:**
- Establish priorities.
- Allocate funding and personnel.
- Resolve disagreements over business criticality.
- Provide visible sponsorship and final approval.

**Exam points:**
- BCP is an **organization-wide responsibility**, not solely an IT/security function.
- Senior management sponsorship is essential.
- Include all business locations, branch offices, and relevant cloud-provider locations.
- Personnel time and effort are significant BCP costs.

### Resource Requirements

| Stage | Main Resource Requirements |
|---|---|
| BCP development | Team effort, planning time, analysis |
| Testing, training, maintenance | Personnel time, testing infrastructure |
| BCP implementation during a disaster | Significant operational effort and financial resources |

## 3. External Dependencies

A BCP must address risks arising from external organizations and obligations.

### Vendors and Service Providers

Examples:
- Cloud providers
- SaaS providers
- Hardware and software vendors
- Telecommunications providers

**Due diligence:**
- Review vendor SLAs and recovery commitments.
- Verify vendor continuity and recovery capabilities.
- Review independent assurance reports.
- Do not rely solely on contractual promises.

### SOC Reports

**SOC = System and Organization Controls**

| Report | Focus |
|---|---|
| SOC 1 | Internal controls over financial reporting |
| SOC 2 | Security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy controls |
| SOC 3 | General-use reporting on the same trust services areas as SOC 2 |

**Exam point:** For evaluating a service provider's security and availability controls, look for **SOC 2 or SOC 3**, not SOC 1.

### Legal, Regulatory, and Contractual Obligations

- Financial institutions, healthcare, emergency services, and other sectors may have continuity requirements.
- SLAs may impose contractual availability and recovery obligations.
- Legal counsel should participate throughout BCP development, testing, and maintenance.
- Requirements vary by jurisdiction and may change over time.

## 4. Business Impact Analysis (BIA)

**BIA:** Identifies critical business processes, evaluates potential disruption impacts, and prioritizes continuity resources.

### Five BIA Steps

1. **Identify priorities:** Determine and rank critical business functions.
2. **Risk identification:** Identify threats that could disrupt operations.
3. **Likelihood assessment:** Determine how frequently threats may occur.
4. **Impact analysis:** Evaluate financial and nonfinancial consequences.
5. **Resource prioritization:** Allocate continuity resources according to business impact.

**Exam distinction:**
- Risk assessment primarily focuses on risks to individual assets.
- BIA primarily focuses on disruption to **business processes and functions**.

### Quantitative vs. Qualitative Analysis

| Quantitative | Qualitative |
|---|---|
| Numerical and financial measures | Nonfinancial or subjective measures |
| AV, EF, SLE, ARO, ALE | Reputation, goodwill, employee retention |
| Uses calculations | Uses judgment and prioritization |

**Both are required.** A financially smaller risk may still deserve high priority because of safety, reputation, or business survival.

## 5. Critical Recovery Metrics

| Metric | Full Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| MTD | Maximum Tolerable Downtime | Maximum disruption duration before irreparable business harm |
| MTO | Maximum Tolerable Outage | Alternative name for MTD |
| RTO | Recovery Time Objective | Target time to restore a business function |
| RPO | Recovery Point Objective | Maximum acceptable data loss expressed as time |

### Key Relationships

**RTO < MTD**

Recovery should occur before the maximum tolerable downtime is reached.

**RTO measures downtime. RPO measures potential data loss.**

### Example

- MTD = 8 hours
- RTO = 4 hours
- RPO = 15 minutes

Interpretation:
- The business cannot tolerate more than 8 hours of disruption.
- The recovery target is 4 hours.
- Up to 15 minutes of data may be lost.

**Exam clues:**
- "How quickly must operations resume?" → RTO
- "How long can the business survive without this function?" → MTD
- "How much data can be lost?" → RPO

## 6. Risk Identification and Likelihood

### Threat Categories

**Natural threats:**
- Earthquakes
- Floods and severe storms
- Wildfires
- Pandemics
- Landslides

**Person-made threats:**
- Terrorism and civil unrest
- Theft and vandalism
- Arson and explosions
- Power outages
- Internet or service-provider failures
- Transportation failures
- Economic crises

**Risk identification:** Determine what could happen.

**Likelihood assessment:** Determine how frequently it might happen.

### Annualized Rate of Occurrence (ARO)

**ARO:** Expected number of occurrences of a threat per year.

Examples:
- Once every year → ARO = 1
- Twice per year → ARO = 2
- Once every 10 years → ARO = 0.1

Sources for estimating likelihood include historical data, experts, insurance data, and government hazard assessments.

## 7. Quantitative Risk Formulas

| Term | Full Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| AV | Asset Value | Monetary value of an asset |
| EF | Exposure Factor | Percentage of asset value lost in one incident |
| SLE | Single Loss Expectancy | Expected monetary loss per incident |
| ARO | Annualized Rate of Occurrence | Expected incidents per year |
| ALE | Annualized Loss Expectancy | Expected annual monetary loss |

### Formulas

**SLE = AV × EF**

**ALE = SLE × ARO**

### Example

A building has:
- AV = $500,000
- EF = 70%
- ARO = 0.03

Calculations:

SLE = $500,000 × 0.70 = **$350,000**

ALE = $350,000 × 0.03 = **$10,500**

Interpretation:
- One incident is expected to cause $350,000 in damage.
- Average expected annual loss is $10,500.

**Exam points:**
- EF is expressed as a percentage or decimal.
- SLE represents a single incident.
- ALE represents expected annual loss.
- ALE helps compare and prioritize risks; it does not mean a loss occurs every year.

### Resource Prioritization

- Rank quantitative risks by ALE, generally highest first.
- Combine quantitative findings with qualitative concerns.
- Senior management and the BCP team determine final priorities.
- Financial calculations alone must not override human safety or critical business needs.

## 8. Continuity Planning

After the BIA, the organization develops strategies to address unacceptable disruption risks.

### Two Activities

1. **Strategy Development**
   - Review prioritized risks.
   - Determine which risks require mitigation.
   - Decide which risks may be accepted.
   - Consider mitigation costs and business impact.

2. **Provisions and Processes**
   - Design specific continuity measures.
   - Protect people, facilities, and infrastructure.

### Continuity of Operations Plan (COOP)

**COOP:** Describes how critical business functions continue shortly after a disruption and through a sustained operating period of up to approximately one month.

### Three Protection Categories

| Category | Continuity Measures |
|---|---|
| People | Safety, evacuation, food, shelter, staffing |
| Buildings and facilities | Hardening, physical protection, alternate sites |
| Infrastructure | Redundancy, backup systems, power protection, alternative communication links |

**Facility hardening:** Protect the existing facility against identified hazards.

**Alternate site:** Provide another location when the primary facility cannot support operations.

**Infrastructure hardening:** Protect existing systems using measures such as UPS and appropriate fire suppression.

**Alternative systems:** Use redundant systems, components, or communication links.

**Exam point:** Outsourcing infrastructure to a cloud provider does not eliminate the organization's continuity dependencies.

## 9. Plan Approval and Implementation

### Plan Approval

- Obtain senior management approval.
- Ideally, the CEO or equivalent senior executive endorses the plan.
- Executive approval demonstrates organizational commitment and supports implementation.

### Plan Implementation

- Establish an implementation schedule.
- Deploy continuity provisions and resources.
- Communicate responsibilities.
- Train personnel.
- Maintain and update the plan.

### Training

- All employees should understand the overall BCP.
- Employees assigned continuity responsibilities require role-specific training.
- Train **at least one backup person for each BCP task**.

**Exam point:** Approval is not the end of BCP. Implementation, training, exercises, and maintenance must follow.

## 10. BCP Documentation

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Continuity Planning Goals | Defines continuity objectives |
| Statement of Importance | Explains why BCP is essential; reinforces executive support |
| Statement of Priorities | Lists critical business functions in priority order |
| Statement of Organizational Responsibility | Establishes shared responsibility for continuity |
| Statement of Urgency and Timing | Defines implementation urgency and schedule |
| Risk Assessment | Documents identified risks and quantitative/qualitative analysis |
| Risk Acceptance/Mitigation | Records accepted risks and mitigation decisions |
| Vital Records Program | Identifies and protects records needed to resume operations |
| Emergency Response Guidelines | Defines immediate actions, responsibilities, and notifications |
| Maintenance | Keeps the plan current |
| Testing and Exercises | Validates the plan and personnel readiness |

### Important Documentation Concepts

**Risk Acceptance:**
- Document the reasons for accepting risks.
- Identify circumstances that may require reassessment.
- Formally record risk acceptance decisions.

**Vital Records Program:**
- Identify records essential to rebuilding or resuming operations.
- Record where those records are stored.
- Define backup and preservation procedures.

**Emergency Response Guidelines:**
- Immediate safety and emergency actions.
- Emergency contact and notification lists.
- Secondary actions while waiting for the BCP team.

---

## 11. BCP Maintenance and Exercises

A BCP is a **living document**.

- Review periodically and after significant organizational changes.
- Update the plan when business processes, resources, or risks change.
- Maintain version control.
- Ensure personnel use the current approved version.
- Review exercise results and correct weaknesses.
- Retain continuity responsibilities within assigned job roles.

### Testing and Exercises

Purpose:
- Validate that continuity procedures work.
- Confirm personnel understand their responsibilities.
- Identify gaps before an actual disaster.
- Keep the plan aligned with organizational needs.

**Note:** Detailed exercise and testing methods are covered in Chapter 18 — Disaster Recovery Planning.

## 12. CISSP Exam — Key Points to Remember

1. **People first:** Human safety takes precedence over systems and business operations.
2. **BCP vs. DRP:** Strategic business continuity vs. tactical technical recovery.
3. **Four BCP phases:** Scope and Planning → BIA → Continuity Planning → Approval and Implementation.
4. **BIA:** Identifies critical business functions and prioritizes continuity requirements.
5. **BIA steps:** Priorities → Risks → Likelihood → Impact → Resource Prioritization.
6. **RTO < MTD:** Recovery target must be shorter than maximum tolerable downtime.
7. **RPO:** Measures acceptable data loss, not recovery duration.
8. **SLE = AV × EF; ALE = SLE × ARO.**
9. **Qualitative impact:** Reputation, trust, workforce, and community responsibilities matter alongside financial losses.
10. **Senior management:** Provides resources, sets priorities, resolves conflicts, and approves the BCP.
11. **External dependencies:** Evaluate vendor continuity capabilities, SLAs, and assurance reports.
12. **SOC 1 vs. SOC 2/3:** Financial reporting controls vs. security and related trust services controls.
13. **COOP:** Supports continued execution of critical functions after disruption.
14. **Continuity protection:** People, buildings/facilities, and infrastructure.
15. **BCP documentation:** Include vital records, emergency procedures, risk decisions, and responsibilities.
16. **Formal risk acceptance:** Document the decision and justification.
17. **Training:** Assign and train a backup for every BCP task.
18. **Maintenance:** BCP must be reviewed, exercised, updated, and version-controlled.
