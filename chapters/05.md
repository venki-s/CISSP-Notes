# CISSP Chapter 5 — Protecting Security of Assets

**Domain 2: Asset Security**  
**Source:** Supplied Official Study Guide Chapter 5 text

---

## A — Concept Understanding Notes

### 1. Asset Security and Data Classification

- **Asset security:** Protect information and the systems/media that hold it throughout the **data lifecycle** (creation/collection → use → storage → retention → destruction).
- **Sensitive data:** Nonpublic information that needs protection because of its value or legal requirements.
- **PII (Personally Identifiable Information):** Data that identifies, distinguishes, traces, or can be linked to a person. **Examples:** Name combined with identifying details, national ID, biometrics, financial/education records.
- **PHI (Protected Health Information):** Individually identifiable health information protected under applicable healthcare privacy rules such as HIPAA. **Example:** A patient's diagnosis and treatment record held by a covered healthcare provider.
> [!WARNING]
> - **Proprietary data:** Information giving an organization a competitive advantage. **Examples:** Source code, product designs, trade secrets.
>
- **Classification:** Assigns a sensitivity/value level to information; the classification determines required handling and security controls.

**Government classification (highest → lowest):**

| Classification | Damage from unauthorized disclosure |
|---|---|
| **Top Secret** | **Exceptionally grave** damage to national security |
| **Secret** | **Serious** damage to national security |
| **Confidential** | **Damage** to national security |
| **Unclassified** | Does not meet classified definitions; some categories still have handling restrictions |

- **CUI / SBU / FOUO:** Unclassified does **not** automatically mean unrestricted distribution.

**Illustrative private-sector classification (highest → lowest in this chapter):**

| Classification | Meaning / Example |
|---|---|
> [!IMPORTANT] 
> | **Confidential / Proprietary** | Greatest protection; unreleased product design or source code |
> | **Private** | Restricted personal/employee information; payroll, PII, PHI |
> | **Sensitive** | Internal data whose exposure causes harm; network diagrams |
> | **Public** | Intended for public access; company website content |
>

- Private organizations can use **different labels and ordering**; follow the organization's approved policy.
- **Asset classification follows data classification:** A laptop, server, or backup tape that processes/stores Top Secret data must be handled as a Top Secret asset.
- **Public data still needs integrity protection**, even when confidentiality is not required.

### 2. Three States of Data

| State | Meaning | Typical protection / Example |
|---|---|---|
| **At rest** | Stored on HDD, SSD, tape, database, or backup | Storage encryption (often symmetric), access control; encrypted database |
| **In transit** | Moving over wired/wireless networks | Transport encryption (e.g., TLS), authentication; HTTPS connection |
| **In use** | Being processed in RAM or temporary buffers | Memory protection, access controls, clear sensitive buffers; application processes a card number |

- Encryption at rest does **not** automatically protect plaintext once an application decrypts it in memory.
- **Homomorphic encryption:** Enables certain computations on encrypted data without first decrypting it.

### 3. Labeling, Handling, and Data Maintenance

- **Labels** communicate the assigned classification: physical tags on media/systems or digital headers, footers, metadata, and watermarks.
- A system processing multiple classifications should be marked/handled according to the **highest classification** it processes.
- **Handling requirements** cover access, sharing, transportation, storage, backups, and disposal, based on classification.
- **Backups and cloud copies need protection equivalent to the original data.** Example: A confidential backup tape is treated as confidential, even off-site.
- **Data maintenance:** Keep data organized and controlled, review policies, and prevent uncontrolled copies of sensitive files.
- **Air gap:** Physical separation of networks; no direct connectivity between classified and unclassified networks.
- **Unidirectional bridge:** Permits data flow in only one direction; **guard solution:** Hardware/software that checks and controls transfers between security domains.
- **Downgrading/reclassification of media** requires an approved sanitization process and may be prohibited for highly classified data.

### 4. Data Lifecycle, Location, and Retention

- **Collection limitation / data minimization:** Collect and keep only information needed for a legitimate purpose. **Example:** A shop uses a payment processor without storing customers' card numbers.
- **Data location:** Know where primary data, copies, and backups reside; keep off-site backups geographically separate from the same likely disaster.
- **Storage protection:** Encryption, physical locks, controlled rooms, and environmental controls (temperature/humidity).
- **Record retention:** Preserve information for the period required by law, business needs, and policy; securely dispose of it afterward.
- **Over-retention:** Raises storage expense, breach exposure, and litigation/discovery obligations.
- **Legal hold:** When litigation is anticipated or underway, **suspend routine deletion** of relevant records even if the retention period has expired.
- **EOL (End of Life):** Vendor stops producing/selling a product; it may still receive support.
- **End of Support:** Vendor stops supporting the product, including routine fixes/updates; reassess or replace unsupported assets.

### 5. Data Loss Prevention (DLP)

- **DLP:** Detects and blocks unauthorized exposure/exfiltration of sensitive data using classification labels, keywords, and patterns (e.g., identification or card-number patterns).

| DLP type | Where / What it controls | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Network DLP** | Outbound network traffic | Blocks an email sending confidential documents outside the company |
| **Endpoint DLP** | Activity on laptops/desktops/servers | Prevents copying a sensitive file to USB or printing it |
| **Cloud DLP** | Sensitive data in cloud services | Detects restricted data uploaded to cloud storage |

- **DLP discovery** finds sensitive data stored in unexpected locations (e.g., PII on employee laptops).
- **Limitation:** DLP generally cannot inspect encrypted content **unless the content is decrypted for inspection**.

### 6. Data Remanence and Secure Destruction

- **Data remanence:** Recoverable information remaining after a file is deleted or storage is supposedly wiped.
- **Slack space:** Unused bytes within an allocated disk cluster; can contain residual information.

| Method | Main meaning | Exam-relevant limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **Delete / erase** | Removes file reference | Data may remain recoverable |
| **Clear** | Overwrite/addressable-area sanitization, typically for reuse | May not reach spare/hidden areas; weaker assurance than purge |
| **Purge** | Stronger sanitization against advanced recovery | Must be suitable for the media and verified |
| **Degauss** | Strong magnetic field disrupts magnetic storage | Works on **magnetic** HDDs/tapes; **not** SSDs, USB flash, or optical discs |
| **Destroy** | Physically destroys media (shred, incinerate, pulverize, etc.) | Highest assurance when media cannot be reused |
| **Cryptographic erase** | Destroys encryption keys, making encrypted content inaccessible | Keys/backups must be unavailable, and encryption must be strong |

- **SSDs:** Degaussing does not work; leftover data in remapped/hidden cells makes ordinary overwriting less reliable. The chapter emphasizes **physical destruction** for highly sensitive SSD disposal.
- **Verification:** Confirm sanitization actually succeeded; a failed wipe or forgotten key backup defeats the purpose.
- **NIST SP 800-88:** Reference for media sanitization methods (clear, purge, destroy).

### 7. Other Data Protection Methods

| Method | Purpose | Simple example |
|---|---|---|
| **DRM (Digital Rights Management)** | Controls authorized use/distribution of copyrighted digital content | An e-book license blocks copying or expires after rental |
| **CASB (Cloud Access Security Broker)** | Enforces security policies between users and cloud services | Enforces cloud encryption, access rules, monitoring, and cloud DLP |
| **Pseudonymization** | Replaces direct identifiers with aliases; identity can be recovered using separately held mapping | Patient name becomes `Patient-23456`; hospital retains lookup table |
| **Tokenization** | Replaces sensitive data with a token linked through a tokenization service/vault | Merchant stores card token rather than card number |
| **Anonymization** | Removes identifying links so the original subject cannot reasonably be identified | Research dataset stripped/transformed to prevent person identification |

- **DRM techniques:** License files, persistent online authentication, audit trails, automatic expiration, anti-copying controls, watermarking.
- **CASB:** Can identify **shadow IT** (unapproved cloud applications/services) using activity and network logs.
- **Pseudonymization is reversible** with additional mapping information; pseudonymized personal data remains reidentifiable.
- **Tokenization:** Original sensitive values are typically held by a protected tokenization service; the token can be used in approved workflows without exposing the original value.
- **Anonymization should not be reversible** when effective; **reidentification/inference** is a risk if combinations of remaining attributes reveal individuals.
- **Randomized masking:** Shuffles attribute values to break associations while potentially preserving aggregate statistics.

### 8. Data Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Primary responsibility | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Data Owner** | Accountable for classification, rules, and appropriate access/protection | Department head classifies payroll data |
| **Data Custodian** | Day-to-day implementation: store, back up, protect, administer | IT administrator maintains backups and access settings |
| **Data Controller** | Decides **why** and **how** personal data is processed | Employer determines purpose of payroll processing |
| **Data Processor** | Processes personal data **on the controller's behalf** | External payroll service runs payroll as instructed |
| **Data User** | Uses authorized data to perform work | Payroll employee accesses approved records |
| **Data Subject** | Individual to whom personal data relates | Employee whose payroll record is processed |

- **Owner is accountable; custodian implements controls.** Delegating implementation does not remove the owner's accountability.
- **Controller determines purposes/means; processor follows controller's instructions.**
- **Data Protection Officer (DPO):** Supports oversight of privacy obligations; the chapter discusses this in the GDPR context.

### 9. Security Baselines, Scoping, and Tailoring

- **Security baseline:** Minimum starting set of controls/configuration requirements for systems.
- **NIST SP 800-53B baselines:** **Low**, **Moderate**, **High** impact, plus a **Privacy** control baseline for personal information.
- **Impact classification** considers possible loss of **Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability (CIA)**; a high impact in any one objective may drive a high-impact baseline.
- **Tailoring:** Adapt the selected baseline to organizational/system needs (add controls, select compensating controls, assign parameters).
  - **Example:** Adjust account lockout threshold or add controls for a sensitive environment.
- **Scoping:** Part of tailoring; determine whether each baseline control actually applies; document why a control can be omitted.
  - **Example:** A concurrent-session restriction may be irrelevant to a system that cannot support concurrent logins.
- **Standards selection:** Identify applicable legal/regulatory/industry requirements when selecting controls. **Examples:** PCI DSS for payment card environments; GDPR when its data-processing scope applies.

## B — CISSP Exam Perspective Notes

### 1. Recognition Clues

| Question wording / scenario | Best concept |

| Restricted but formally unclassified information | **CUI / SBU / FOUO** |

| Device holds several classification levels | **Protect at highest classification** |
| Protect publicly readable website content from modification | **Integrity of public data** |
| Sensitive information in RAM | **Data in use** |
| HTTPS traffic crossing a network | **Data in transit** |
| Encrypted SSD/database/backup | **Data at rest** |
| Discover files containing SSNs; stop outbound leakage | **DLP** |
| Block USB copying or printing | **Endpoint DLP** |
| Enforce company controls on cloud apps; discover shadow IT | **CASB** |
| Prevent unauthorized e-book copying/use | **DRM** |
| Replace patient name with an alias and retain lookup table | **Pseudonymization** |
| Replace payment card number with vault-backed reference | **Tokenization** |
| Remove links so person cannot reasonably be identified | **Anonymization** |
| Retrieve deleted data from storage | **Data remanence** |
| Erase magnetic tapes with powerful magnetic field | **Degaussing** |
| Sanitize SSD or flash media using degaussing | **Wrong method** |
| Encrypted data made inaccessible by deleting keys | **Cryptographic erasure** |
| Keep litigation-related records beyond normal deletion date | **Legal hold** |

| Vendor stops security/support updates | **End of Support** |
| Who determines data classification/access? | **Data Owner** |
| Who performs backups and technical protections? | **Data Custodian** |
| Who decides why/how personal data is processed? | **Data Controller** |
| Who processes personal data for another party? | **Data Processor** |
| Set minimum controls for low/moderate/high impact | **Security baseline** |
| Adapt baseline and add compensating controls | **Tailoring** |
| Justifiably omit controls not applicable to a system | **Scoping** |

### 2. High-Value Distinctions

1. **Data owner vs. custodian:** Owner **decides and is accountable**; custodian **implements and maintains** safeguards.
2. **Controller vs. processor:** Controller determines **why/how**; processor performs processing **for the controller**.
3. **Classification vs. labeling:** Classification **assigns sensitivity**; labeling **communicates the assigned sensitivity**.
4. **DLP vs. CASB vs. DRM:** DLP stops **data leakage**; CASB enforces **cloud-use policies**; DRM controls **digital content rights**.
5. **Pseudonymization vs. anonymization:** Pseudonymization allows **reidentification with a key/mapping**; effective anonymization **does not**.
6. **Tokenization vs. encryption:** Tokenization **substitutes a reference token**; encryption **transforms data into ciphertext using a key**.
7. **Clear vs. purge vs. destroy:** Clearing supports routine reuse; purging provides **higher recovery resistance**; destruction prevents media reuse.
8. **Degaussing vs. SSD:** Degaussing applies to **magnetic storage only**; it does **not** sanitize SSDs.
9. **Tailoring vs. scoping:** Tailoring is the **overall adaptation** of a baseline; scoping checks **applicability** and justifies omissions.
10. **Retention vs. legal hold:** Normally delete at the end of retention; **preserve relevant records when subject to legal hold**.
11. **EOL vs. end of support:** EOL ends sale/production; end of support ends vendor assistance/updates.
12. **Public vs. unprotected:** Public information may not need confidentiality, but **integrity and availability still matter**.

### 3. Short Exam Rules to Remember

- **Classify first → label → apply handling/protection rules → retain as required → sanitize/destroy.**
- **Keep only what is needed**: collecting less personal data reduces breach exposure.
- **Protect copies, backups, and cloud data at the same classification** as the original.
- **The value of sensitive information generally exceeds the value of its storage medium.**
- **Do not assume Delete = Secure Erase.** Always consider data remanence.
- **Do not downgrade classified media without an approved sanitization procedure.**
- **Do not destroy records under a legal hold**, even if ordinary retention rules would delete them.
- **One high CIA impact can justify a high-impact control baseline.**
- **Document why a baseline control is omitted during scoping.**
