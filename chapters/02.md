
# CISSP Chapter 2 — Personnel Security and Risk Management Concepts

**Domain:** 1 — Security and Risk Management  
**Source:** Sybex CISSP Official Study Guide, 10th Edition

## 1. Personnel Security

- **Job Description:** Defines responsibilities and guides access privilege assignments.
- **Candidate Screening:** Background checks should be proportional to job sensitivity and comply with applicable laws.
- **Onboarding:** Employment agreements, AUP, NDA, security training, and account provisioning.
- **Least Privilege:** Grant only the minimum access required to perform assigned duties.
- **Privilege Creep:** Accumulation of unnecessary privileges over time; addressed through periodic access reviews.
- **Mandatory Vacation:** Helps detect fraud or misconduct when another employee performs the absent worker's duties.
- **Separation of Duties:** Prevents one individual from controlling an entire sensitive process.
- **Job Rotation:** Helps detect irregularities and reduces dependency on individuals.
- <mark class="confident"> **Collusion:** Two or more individuals cooperate to commit fraud or bypass controls.</mark>
- <mark class="confident"> **UBA:** User Behavior Analytics. </mark>
- <mark class="confident"> **UEBA:** User and Entity Behavior Analytics; includes devices, systems, and applications. </mark>

<mark class="confident"> ### Employment Agreements </mark>

- **AUP:** Defines acceptable and prohibited use of organizational resources.
- **NDA:** Protects confidential information; obligations may continue after employment.
  - **Unilateral:** One party protects another's information.
  - **Bilateral:** Two parties mutually protect information.
  - **Multilateral:** Three or more parties.
- **Non-compete Agreement:** Restricts competitive activities; enforceability varies by jurisdiction.

### Offboarding and Transfers

- Revoke access and privileges when employment ends.
- Recover company equipment, badges, keys, and tokens.
- Revoke certificates and physical access.
- Disable accounts before eventual deletion when audit retention is required.
- During transfers, remove obsolete privileges before assigning new ones.

## 2. Third-Party Security

- **SLA:** Defines expected service levels, performance requirements, and remedies.
- **Outsourcing:** May transfer certain risks but does not eliminate organizational accountability.
- **Multiparty Risk:** Organizations may have conflicting objectives, controls, and risk priorities.
- **VMS:** Vendor Management System; supports centralized vendor management.
- Third parties introduce supply-chain, confidentiality, compliance, and operational risks.

<mark class="confident"> ## 3. Risk Terminology </mark>

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Asset | Anything of value to an organization |
| Threat | Potential cause of harm |
| Threat Actor | Entity intentionally exploiting vulnerabilities |
| Threat Event | Occurrence capable of causing harm |
| Threat Vector | Path or method used to reach a target |
| Vulnerability | Weakness that can be exploited |
| Exposure | Condition of being susceptible to loss |
| Risk | Likelihood and impact of harm |
| Safeguard | Control used to reduce risk |
| Attack | Attempt to exploit a vulnerability |
| Breach | Successful compromise of security |
| Hazard | Potential source of harm |

**Risk = Likelihood × Impact**

## 4. Risk Assessment

- **Risk Assessment:** Identifies, analyzes, and prioritizes risks.
- **Risk Response:** Determines how identified risks will be treated.
- **Scope:** Defines boundaries of the assessment.
- **Asset Valuation:** Includes replacement, operational, intellectual property, productivity, and liability costs.
- **Asset-Based Assessment:** Identify assets first, then associated threats.
- **Threat-Based Assessment:** Identify threats first, then affected assets.

### Qualitative vs. Quantitative

| Qualitative | Quantitative |
|---|---|
| Subjective | Numerical |
| Low / Medium / High | Financial values and probabilities |
| Relies on judgment | Uses calculations |
| Faster and simpler | More detailed and resource-intensive |

- **Hybrid Analysis:** Combines qualitative and quantitative approaches.
- <mark class="confident"> **Delphi Technique:** Anonymous, iterative feedback to achieve consensus while reducing bias. </mark>

## 5. Quantitative Risk Analysis

| Term | Formula / Meaning |
|---|---|
| AV | Asset Value |
| EF | Exposure Factor — percentage lost in one event |
| SLE | Single Loss Expectancy = AV × EF |
| ARO | Annualized Rate of Occurrence |
| ALE | Annualized Loss Expectancy = SLE × ARO |
| ACS | Annual Cost of Safeguard |

**Important Formulas:**

- `SLE = AV × EF`
- `ALE = SLE × ARO`
- `ALE = AV × EF × ARO`
- `Safeguard Benefit = (ALE Before − ALE After) − ACS`

**Example:**

- AV = $200,000
- EF = 25%
- ARO = 0.5
- SLE = $50,000
- ALE = $25,000

If a safeguard reduces ALE to $5,000 and costs $8,000 annually:

`Benefit = $25,000 − $5,000 − $8,000 = $12,000`

**Exam Point:** A positive safeguard benefit indicates financial value, but legal and regulatory obligations must also be considered.

<mark class="confident"> ## 6. Risk Responses </mark>

| Response | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mitigation | Reduce likelihood or impact |
| Transfer / Assignment | Shift financial consequences, e.g., insurance |
| Deterrence | Discourage an attack |
| Avoidance | Eliminate the risk-producing activity |
| Acceptance | Knowingly retain risk with management approval |
| Rejection / Ignoring | Disregard risk; not an acceptable risk-management practice |

### Risk Levels

- **Inherent Risk:** Risk before controls.
- **Residual Risk:** Risk remaining after controls.
- **Control Risk:** Risk introduced by a security control.
- **Risk Appetite:** Overall risk an organization is willing to accept.
- **Risk Capacity:** Maximum risk the organization can withstand.
- **Risk Tolerance:** Acceptable risk for a particular situation.
- **Risk Target:** Preferred risk level.
- **Risk Limit:** Maximum permitted deviation above the target.

**Residual Risk = Total Risk − Risk Reduction**

**Exam Point:** Senior management is responsible for risk acceptance. Security professionals identify, assess, and recommend treatments.

<mark class="confident"> ## 7. Security Controls </mark>

### Control Categories

- **Administrative:** Policies, procedures, background checks, training.
- **Technical / Logical:** Authentication, encryption, firewalls, access controls.
- **Physical:** Guards, fences, locks, access control vestibules.

### Control Functions

| Control | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Preventive | Stop an incident | Firewall |
| Detective | Discover an incident | IDS, audit logs |
| Corrective | Fix a problem | Malware removal |
| Recovery | Restore operations | Backup restoration |
| Deterrent | Discourage violations | Warning signs |
| Directive | Instruct required behavior | Security policy |
| Compensating | Alternative protection | Additional monitoring |

- **Defense in Depth:** Multiple overlapping controls.
- **SCA:** Security Control Assessment; evaluates control effectiveness.
- **Continuous Monitoring:** Measures whether controls remain effective.
- **Cost-Effective Security:** Select controls based on risk reduction, cost, and business requirements.

<mark class="confident"> ## 8. Risk Reporting, Maturity, and Frameworks </mark>

- **Risk Register:** Records risks, owners, priorities, responses, and status.
- **Risk Heat Map:** Displays risks using likelihood and impact.
- **Internal Reporting:** Supports management decisions.
- **External Reporting:** Supports regulatory and stakeholder obligations.
- **Continuous Improvement:** Reassess risks as conditions change.

### Risk Maturity Model (RMM)

1. **Ad Hoc:** Informal, chaotic processes.
2. **Preliminary:** Inconsistent departmental processes.
3. **Defined:** Standardized organization-wide framework.
4. **Integrated:** Risk management embedded in business processes.
5. **Optimized:** Continuous improvement.


### NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0

Six functions:

1. **Govern** – Define security policies, responsibilities, and risk management strategy.
2. **Identify** – Identify assets, vulnerabilities, and cybersecurity risks.
3. **Protect** – Implement safeguards such as MFA, encryption, and access controls.
4. **Detect** – Identify security threats, anomalies, and incidents.
5. **Respond** – Contain, investigate, and mitigate security incidents.
6. **Recover** – Restore affected systems and resume normal operations.

### NIST Risk Management Framework (RMF)

Seven steps:

1. **Prepare** to execute the RMF from an organization- and system-level perspective by establishing a context and priorities for managing security and privacy risk.
2. **Categorize** the system and the information processed, stored, and transmitted by the system based on an analysis of the impact of loss.
3. **Select** an initial set of controls for the system and tailor the controls as needed to reduce risk to an acceptable level based on an assessment of risk.
4. **Implement** the controls and describe how the controls are employed within the system and its environment of operation.
5. **Assess** the controls to determine if the controls are implemented correctly, operating as intended, and producing the desired outcomes with respect to satisfying the security and privacy requirements.
6. **Authorize** the system or common controls based on a determination that the risk to organizational operations and assets, individuals, other organizations, and the Nation is acceptable.
7. **Monitor** the system and the associated controls on an ongoing basis to include assessing control effectiveness, documenting changes to the system and environment of operation, conducting risk assessments and impact analyses, and reporting the security and privacy posture of the system.


### Other Frameworks

- **ISO 31000:** Enterprise risk management.
- **ISO 27005:** Information security risk management.
- **COBIT:** IT governance and management.
- **SABSA:** Business-driven security architecture.
- **PCI DSS:** Payment card data security.
- **OCTAVE:** Organizational risk assessment.
- **FAIR:** Quantitative information risk analysis.
- **TARA:** Threat Agent Risk Assessment.

### Legacy Systems

- **EOL:** End of Life; manufacturing may stop while support continues.
- **EOS / EOSL:** End of Support / End of Service Life; vendor support or updates cease.

<mark class="confident"> ## 9. Social Engineering </mark>

### Psychological Principles

- **Authority:** Pretending to have legitimate power.
- **Intimidation:** Threatening consequences.
- **Consensus / Social Proof:** Claiming others already comply.
- **Scarcity:** Limited availability.
- **Familiarity:** Appearing known or relatable.
- **Trust:** Building a relationship to exploit it.
- **Urgency:** Pressuring immediate action.


### Social Engineering Attacks — CISSP Exam Notes

| Attack | Simple Explanation / Exam Clue | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Phishing** | Mass deceptive messages sent to many people. | Fake bank email asking users to reset passwords. |
| **Spear Phishing** | Personalized phishing targeting specific individuals or groups. | Email targeting a Cisco employee using their name and project details. |
| **Whaling** | Spear phishing targeting executives or high-value individuals. | Fake legal notice sent to a CEO requesting confidential information. |
| **Smishing** | Phishing through SMS/text messages. | SMS claiming a parcel is delayed, with a malicious tracking link. |
| **Vishing** | Phishing through voice calls. | Caller pretending to be bank support requesting an OTP. |
| **Pretexting** | Creating a believable fabricated story to gain trust or information. | Attacker pretends to be IT support investigating a security issue. |
| **Elicitation** | Extracting sensitive information through seemingly innocent conversation. | Casually asking an employee about internal systems during a conference. |
| **Prepending** | Adding misleading prefixes to make messages appear legitimate or familiar. | Fake email subject "RE: Approved Salary Revision" to encourage opening. |
| **Impersonation** | Pretending to be a trusted person or authority. | Attacker poses as a company administrator to obtain credentials. |
| **Tailgating** | Following an authorized person into a restricted area without their knowledge or permission. | Sneaking through a secure door immediately behind an employee. |
| **Piggybacking** | Entering a restricted area with someone's permission, often obtained through deception. | Pretending to have forgotten an access card and asking an employee to let them in. |
| **Baiting** | Offering something attractive to trick victims into unsafe actions. | Leaving an infected USB drive labeled "Confidential Salary Data." |
| **Shoulder Surfing** | Secretly observing someone entering sensitive information. | Watching a person type their ATM PIN. |
| **Dumpster Diving** | Searching discarded materials for confidential information. | Retrieving printed passwords or customer records from office trash. |
| **Typosquatting** | Registering lookalike domains based on common typing mistakes. | Using `gooogle.com` instead of `google.com` to deceive visitors. |
| **BEC (Business Email Compromise)** | Fraudulent business communication, often involving impersonation or compromised accounts. | Fake CFO email instructing Finance to transfer money to an attacker. |
| **Hoax** | False warning or fabricated information intended to cause fear or unsafe behavior. | Fake virus alert instructing employees to disable antivirus software. |
| **Influence Campaign** | Coordinated efforts to manipulate public opinion or behavior using information. | Fake social media accounts spreading misleading narratives. |
| **Hybrid Warfare** | Combining cyberattacks, disinformation, political pressure, and conventional military tactics. | Cyberattacks on infrastructure combined with propaganda and military operations. |

### Important CISSP Exam Distinctions

- **Phishing vs. Spear Phishing:** Mass audience vs. specifically targeted victims.
- **Spear Phishing vs. Whaling:** Targeted individuals vs. high-value executives.
- **Smishing vs. Vishing:** SMS/text messages vs. voice calls.
- **Pretexting vs. Elicitation:** Fabricated scenario vs. conversational information extraction.
- **Impersonation vs. Pretexting:** False identity vs. fabricated story; both may occur together.
- **Tailgating vs. Piggybacking:** Without permission vs. with permission (common CISSP study distinction; terminology can vary).
- **Baiting vs. Phishing:** Attractive object/reward vs. deceptive communication.
- **Typosquatting vs. Phishing:** Lookalike domain vs. deceptive message; often used together.
- **BEC vs. Whaling:** Business transaction fraud vs. executive-targeted phishing.
- **Influence Campaign vs. Hybrid Warfare:** Opinion manipulation vs. coordinated use of multiple warfare tactics.

**Exam Tip:** Identify the attack based on its delivery method, target, deception technique, or intended outcome. A single attack can involve multiple social engineering techniques.


**Defenses:** Awareness, identity verification, information classification, reporting, and physical access controls.

## 10. Security Awareness, Training, and Education

| Activity | Objective |
|---|---|
| Awareness | Recognize security risks and responsibilities |
| Training | Learn job-specific security skills |
| Education | Develop deeper security knowledge |

- **Security Champions:** Employees promoting secure practices within teams.
- **Gamification:** Rewards, scores, and challenges to improve engagement.
- **Micro-Training:** Short, focused learning modules.
- **Phishing Simulations:** Evaluate susceptibility and training effectiveness.
- **Role-Based Training:** Tailored to specific job responsibilities.
- **Effectiveness Evaluation:** Measure retention, behavior, and incident trends.
- **Periodic Review:** Update training for emerging threats and technologies.

## CISSP Exam — Final Recall Points

1. **Management accepts risk; security teams recommend responses.**
2. **Risk assessment identifies and prioritizes; risk response selects treatment.**
3. **SLE = AV × EF; ALE = SLE × ARO.**
4. **Positive safeguard benefit indicates financial value, not automatic approval.**
5. **Risk transfer does not eliminate organizational accountability.**
6. **Residual risk remains after safeguards are implemented.**
7. **Preventive stops; detective identifies; corrective fixes; recovery restores.**
8. **Tailgating = without knowledge; piggybacking = consent through deception** (chapter terminology).
9. **Delphi = anonymous consensus.**
10. **NIST CSF 2.0 = 6 functions; NIST RMF = 7 steps.**
11. **Awareness = recognition; training = skills; education = deeper knowledge.**
12. **Risk management is continuous, not a one-time activity.**
