
# CISSP Chapter 4 — Laws, Regulations, and Compliance

**Sybex Official Study Guide, 10th Edition | Domain 1: Security and Risk Management**  
**CISSP Objective:** 1.4 — Legal, Regulatory, and Compliance Issues  
**Andrew's Course:** Section 5 — Understand Legal, Regulatory, and Compliance Issues

## A — Core Concepts

### 1. Categories of Law

| Category | Purpose | Typical Outcome | CISSP Clue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Criminal** | Government prosecutes prohibited conduct | Fines, imprisonment | Computer crime, law enforcement |
| **Civil** | Injured party brings a lawsuit | Damages, injunctions | Contracts, compensation, disputes |
| **Administrative** | Government agencies issue and enforce regulations | Regulatory penalties | Agency rules and regulations |

- **USC (United States Code):** Federal criminal and civil statutes.
- **CFR (Code of Federal Regulations):** Federal administrative regulations.
- Administrative regulations must comply with statutes and constitutional requirements.
- **Exam principle:** Identify applicable laws and jurisdictions; involve qualified legal counsel for legal interpretations.

### 2. U.S. Cybercrime and Government Security Laws

| Law | CISSP-Relevant Purpose |
|---|---|
| **CFAA — Computer Fraud and Abuse Act** | Unauthorized computer access, fraud, and computer damage |
| **1994 CFAA Amendments** | Expanded coverage to interstate commerce; addressed malicious code and civil remedies |
| **NIIPA — National Information Infrastructure Protection Act (1996)** | Extended protection to international commerce and critical infrastructure |
| **FISMA (2002)** | Requires federal agencies to establish information security programs, including contractor activities |
| **Federal Information Security Modernization Act (2014)** | Updated FISMA and federal cybersecurity responsibilities |
| **Cybersecurity Enhancement Act (2014)** | NIST coordination of voluntary cybersecurity standards |
| **National Cybersecurity Protection Act (2014)** | National coordination and sharing of cybersecurity threats and incidents |
| **Identity Theft and Assumption Deterrence Act** | Recognizes the individual whose identity was stolen as the victim |

**FISMA Security Program Requirements:**

- Periodic risk assessments.
- Risk-based security policies and controls.
- System security plans.
- Security awareness training.
- Periodic security control testing (at least annually).
- Remediation of identified deficiencies.
- Incident detection, reporting, and response.
- Business continuity planning.

**NIST References:**

| Standard | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **NIST SP 800-53** | Security and privacy controls for information systems and organizations |
| **NIST SP 800-171** | Protecting Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) in nonfederal systems |
| **NIST CSF** | Risk-based cybersecurity framework |

**CFAA Exam Distinction:**

*Van Buren v. United States (2021)* distinguishes misuse of information someone is authorized to access from obtaining information in areas they are not authorized to access.

### 3. Intellectual Property (IP)

| Protection | What It Protects | Duration / Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| **Copyright** | Original expression, including software source code | Automatic on creation; generally life + 70 years |
| **Trademark** | Brand names, slogans, and logos | 10 years for U.S. registration; renewable |
| **Utility Patent** | How an invention functions | Generally 20 years from filing |
| **Design Patent** | Ornamental appearance of an invention | 15 years in the U.S. |
| **Trade Secret** | Confidential business information | Continues while secrecy and reasonable safeguards are maintained |

**Copyright:**

- Protects expression, not the underlying idea or process.
- Software source code qualifies for copyright protection.
- Registration is not required for copyright to arise.
- **Work for hire:** Copyright may belong to the employer or contracting party when legal requirements are met.
- Works for hire: 95 years from publication or 120 years from creation, whichever is shorter.

**DMCA — Digital Millennium Copyright Act:**

- Addresses circumvention of copyright protection mechanisms.
- Provides conditional liability limitations for service providers.
- Includes notice-and-removal provisions.
- Permits certain software backup and maintenance copying activities.

**Trademarks:**

- **™:** Unregistered trademark designation.
- **®:** Registered trademark.
- Primary purpose: Prevent consumer confusion.

**Patents:**

Three requirements:

1. **Novel:** New invention.
2. **Useful:** Performs a useful function.
3. **Nonobvious:** Not an obvious solution.

**Trade Secrets:**

- No registration.
- Protect through need-to-know access, confidentiality controls, and NDAs.
- Protection may be lost if secrecy is not maintained.

**Economic Espionage Act (1996):**

- Criminal protection against trade secret theft.
- Distinguishes theft benefiting foreign governments from other trade secret theft.

**Defend Trade Secrets Act (2016):**

- Added a federal civil cause of action for trade secret theft.

### 4. Software Licensing

| License | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Perpetual** | One-time purchase; indefinite use subject to terms |
| **Subscription** | Recurring payment; time-limited rights |
| **Open Source** | Use, modification, and redistribution subject to license conditions |
| **Freeware** | Free of charge, but may remain proprietary |
| **ELA** | Enterprise License Agreement |
| **EULA** | End-User License Agreement |
| **Concurrent Use** | Limits simultaneous users |
| **Named User** | Assigned to specific users |
| **Cloud/SaaS** | Rights and obligations defined by cloud service agreements |

**Exam Trap:**

- Open source does not mean unrestricted use.
- Freeware does not necessarily mean open source.

### 5. Import and Export Controls

| Regulation | Coverage | List |
|---|---|---|
| **ITAR** | Military and defense articles and technical information | **USML** — United States Munitions List |
| **EAR** | Commercial and dual-use technologies | **CCL** — Commerce Control List |

- **BIS:** Bureau of Industry and Security.
- Encryption products may be subject to export controls.
- Export eligibility depends on destination, end user, end use, technology, and licensing requirements.

**Exam Clue:**

- Military/defense technology → **ITAR**
- Commercial technology with military applications → **EAR**


### 6. U.S. Privacy and Sector-Specific Laws

| Law | Full Form | Primary Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Fourth Amendment | Fourth Amendment to the U.S. Constitution | Protects against unreasonable government searches and seizures |
| Privacy Act of 1974 | Already the full name | Protects personal records maintained by U.S. federal agencies |
| ECPA (1986) | Electronic Communications Privacy Act | Protects electronic communications against unauthorized interception and access |
| CALEA (1994) | Communications Assistance for Law Enforcement Act | Requires telecommunications carriers to support lawful interception |
| HIPAA (1996) | Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act | Healthcare information privacy and security |
| HITECH (2009) | Health Information Technology for Economic and Clinical Health Act | Strengthened HIPAA, business associate obligations, and breach notification |
| COPPA (1998) | Children's Online Privacy Protection Act | Protects children's online privacy; parental consent requirements for children under 13 |
| GLBA (1999) | Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act | Privacy and security of customer financial information |
| FERPA (1974) | Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act | Protects student education records |
| USA PATRIOT Act (2001) | Uniting and Strengthening America by Providing Appropriate Tools Required to Intercept and Obstruct Terrorism Act | Expanded certain investigation and surveillance powers |
| CLOUD Act (2018) | Clarifying Lawful Overseas Use of Data Act | Lawful access to electronic data across national borders |
| California SB 1386 | California Senate Bill 1386 | Early U.S. state data breach notification law |


**HIPAA vs. HITECH:**

- **HIPAA:** Protects PHI (Protected Health Information).
- **HITECH:** Strengthened HIPAA and introduced additional breach notification requirements.
- **BAA:** Business Associate Agreement.
- Business associates are directly subject to relevant HIPAA obligations.
- The chapter discusses notification requirements for breaches affecting more than 500 individuals and a 60-day outer notification deadline.

**COPPA vs. FERPA:**

- COPPA → Children's online personal information.
- FERPA → Student education records.

**GLBA vs. HIPAA:**

- GLBA → Financial information.
- HIPAA → Healthcare information.

**Privacy Act of 1974:**

Applies to **U.S. federal agencies**, not generally to private companies.

**Workplace Monitoring:**

- Employer-owned equipment may have reduced expectations of privacy.
- Use acceptable-use policies, employment terms, and logon banners.
- Consult legal counsel before implementing employee monitoring.

### 7. International Privacy Laws

| Jurisdiction | Law | Primary Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **European Union** | GDPR | Personal data protection and accountability |
| **Canada** | PIPEDA | Personal information handled by commercial organizations |
| **China** | PIPL | Personal information processing and cross-border restrictions |
| **South Africa** | POPIA | Lawful processing and protection of personal information |
| **California** | CCPA | Consumer privacy rights |

**GDPR — Seven Principles:**

1. **Lawfulness, Fairness, and Transparency**
2. **Purpose Limitation**
3. **Data Minimization**
4. **Accuracy**
5. **Storage Limitation**
6. **Integrity and Confidentiality**
7. **Accountability**

**GDPR Exam Concepts:**

- Can apply to organizations outside the EU.
- Requires a lawful basis for processing personal information.
- Individuals have rights concerning their personal data.
- Organizations must demonstrate compliance.
- International data transfers require an appropriate legal mechanism.

**Cross-Border Data Transfers:**

| Mechanism | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **SCC — Standard Contractual Clauses** | Approved contractual terms for certain international transfers |
| **BCR — Binding Corporate Rules** | Approved rules for transfers within multinational corporate groups |
| **Schrems II (2020)** | Invalidated the former EU–U.S. Privacy Shield |
| **APEC Privacy Framework** | Privacy principles supporting cross-border information flows |

**Important:** The Sybex chapter's discussion of successor EU–U.S. transfer arrangements is dated. Do not treat it as an exhaustive statement of current law.

**CCPA Consumer Rights:**

- Right to know.
- Right to request deletion.
- Right to opt out of the sale of personal information.
- Right to exercise privacy rights without discrimination.

### 8. Compliance Requirements

| Requirement | Source |
|---|---|
| **Law** | Legislature |
| **Regulation** | Authorized government agency |
| **Contract** | Agreement between parties |
| **Industry Standard** | Industry body; may become mandatory through contracts or other obligations |

**PCI DSS — Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard**

- Primarily an **industry/contractual compliance requirement**, not itself a law.
- Applies to relevant organizations handling payment-card data, including service providers.

**PCI DSS 4.0 — 12 Requirements:**

1. Install and maintain network security controls.
2. Apply secure configurations.
3. Protect stored account data.
4. Protect cardholder data during transmission.
5. Protect systems against malware.
6. Develop and maintain secure systems and software.
7. Restrict access by business need to know.
8. Identify users and authenticate access.
9. Restrict physical access.
10. Log and monitor access.
11. Test security regularly.
12. Maintain security policies and programs.

**SOX — Sarbanes–Oxley Act:**

- Relevant to financial reporting and supporting IT controls.
- IT control audits may be performed to support SOX compliance.

**Compliance Governance:**

- Identify applicable obligations.
- Assess controls.
- Remediate gaps.
- Maintain compliance evidence.
- Perform required audits.
- Report compliance status to leadership and relevant stakeholders.

### 9. Vendor and Cloud Security

Security assessments should occur during vendor selection and throughout the relationship.

**Important Vendor Review Questions:**

- What sensitive information does the vendor store, process, or transmit?
- What security controls protect the information?
- How is customer data segregated?
- What encryption and key-management controls are used?
- Are independent security audit results available?
- Does the vendor use subcontractors or subprocessors?
- Where is the data stored and processed?
- What are the incident response and breach notification obligations?
- How are integrity, availability, and continuity maintained?

## B — CISSP Exam Traps / Fast Recall

1. **Criminal vs. Civil:** Government prosecution vs. private lawsuit. Both may arise from the same incident.
2. **Administrative vs. Statutory:** Agency regulations (CFR) vs. federal statutes (USC).
3. **CFAA vs. FISMA:** Computer misuse and unauthorized access vs. federal agency security programs.
4. **NIST 800-53 vs. 800-171:** Security/privacy controls vs. CUI protection in nonfederal systems.
5. **Copyright vs. Patent:** Expression/source code vs. invention/function.
6. **Trademark vs. Trade Secret:** Brand identifier vs. confidential business information.
7. **Trade Secret:** No registration; protection requires ongoing secrecy and safeguards.
8. **ITAR vs. EAR:** Defense-specific vs. commercial/dual-use.
9. **Privacy Act 1974:** Federal agencies, not all private organizations.
10. **HIPAA vs. HITECH:** Healthcare information protection vs. enhancements, business associates, and breach notification.
11. **COPPA vs. FERPA:** Children's online data (under 13) vs. education records.
12. **GLBA vs. HIPAA:** Financial information vs. healthcare PHI.
13. **GDPR vs. CCPA:** EU personal-data principles vs. California consumer privacy rights.
14. **SCC vs. BCR:** Contractual transfer clauses vs. approved intra-group transfer rules.
15. **CLOUD Act:** Provider control over data may matter more than physical server location.
16. **PCI DSS:** Industry/contractual standard, not a federal statute.
17. **Vendor Governance:** Evaluate data location, subvendors, audits, encryption, breach notification, and availability.
18. **Managerial Perspective:** Identify applicable obligations, consult legal counsel where necessary, implement appropriate controls, and document compliance.
