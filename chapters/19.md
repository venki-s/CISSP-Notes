## 1. Types of Investigations

| Type | Purpose / Standard |
|---|---|
| Administrative | Internal policy violations, employee discipline, and operational issues |
| Criminal | Violation of criminal law; Beyond a Reasonable Doubt |
| Civil | Disputes between parties; Preponderance of the Evidence |
| Regulatory | Violations of administrative laws or regulations |
| Industry Standards | Contractual compliance violations, e.g., PCI DSS |

### Important
- **Beyond a Reasonable Doubt:** Highest standard of proof among those listed; used in criminal cases.
- **Preponderance of Evidence:** More likely than not (>50%); commonly used in civil cases.
- Administrative investigations may escalate into criminal investigations.
- Operational investigations generally have less rigorous evidence requirements than investigations leading to legal or disciplinary action.
- Industry standards such as PCI DSS create contractual obligations, not criminal laws.

## 2. Electronic Discovery (eDiscovery)

- Process of preserving, identifying, collecting, and producing electronic information for legal proceedings.
- Requires coordination with legal counsel.

### Electronic Discovery Reference Model (EDRM)

1. **Information Governance:** Manage information.
2. **Identification:** Locate relevant information.
3. **Preservation:** Prevent alteration or deletion.
4. **Collection:** Gather information.
5. **Processing:** Filter irrelevant data.
6. **Review:** Determine relevance and privilege.
7. **Analysis:** Examine content and context.
8. **Production:** Deliver information to legal parties.
9. **Presentation:** Present evidence in legal proceedings.

## 3. Evidence Admissibility and Types

### Three Requirements for Admissibility
- **Relevant:** Helps establish or disprove a fact.
- **Material:** Fact is significant to the case.
- **Competent:** Obtained legally.

### Four Types of Evidence

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Real | Physical objects, e.g., computer or hard drive |
| Documentary | Written/electronic records, e.g., logs |
| Testimonial | Witness statements under oath |
| Demonstrative | Diagrams or models used to explain testimony |

### Evidence Rules

**Best Evidence Rule**
- Generally requires original documentary evidence.
- Copies may be admitted under recognized exceptions.

**Parol Evidence Rule**
- A final written agreement is generally treated as containing its terms.
- Prior or contemporaneous verbal agreements generally cannot contradict the integrated written contract.

**Hearsay Rule**
- Generally restricts out-of-court statements offered to prove their truth.
- Exceptions exist.

**Business Records Exception**
- Records routinely created and maintained during normal business operations may be admissible.
- Relevant to computer logs and audit records.
- Requires appropriate authentication.

### Witnesses
- Ordinary witnesses testify based on personal knowledge.
- Qualified expert witnesses may provide professional opinions.

## 4. Chain of Custody

### Purpose
- Establishes evidence authenticity, integrity, and accountability.
- Documents every transfer and person handling the evidence.

### Documentation
- Evidence description.
- Collection date, time, and location.
- Name of collector.
- Collection circumstances.
- Every custodian, transfer time, and reason for access.

### Important
- Maintain an unbroken chain of custody.
- Protect evidence against alteration or unauthorized access.
- Inadequate documentation can undermine evidentiary value.

## 5. Digital Forensics

### Core Principles
- Preserve original evidence.
- Avoid altering evidence during collection.
- Use qualified forensic personnel.
- Document all evidence-handling activities.
- Analyze verified copies rather than originals.

### Forensic Disk Imaging
1. Secure the original storage device.
2. Connect through a **write blocker**.
3. Calculate the original media hash.
4. Create a bit-for-bit forensic image.
5. Calculate and compare image hashes.
6. Preserve original evidence and analyze working copies.

### Volatile Evidence
- RAM contents are lost when power is removed.
- Capture volatile memory before shutdown when feasible.
- Use trusted collection tools and preserve cryptographic hashes.

### Forensic Analysis Types
- **Media Analysis:** Storage devices, deleted files, disk images.
- **Network Analysis:** Packet captures, flow data, firewall and IDS/IPS logs.
- **Software Analysis:** Application code, logs, malicious changes, file hashes.
- **Hardware Analysis:** Computers, mobile devices, embedded systems.

### Locard's Exchange Principle
- **Every contact leaves a trace.**
- Digital interactions may leave evidence on hosts, networks, and applications.

### Important
- Write blocker → Prevent modification of original storage.
- Cryptographic hash → Verify evidence integrity.
- Forensic image → Bitwise copy of storage.
- Network tap/SPAN → Capture network activity.
- Live memory acquisition → Preserve volatile evidence.

## 6. Investigation Procedures and Legal Authority

### Investigation Preparation
- Define scope, authority, roles, and rules of engagement.
- Involve legal counsel when necessary.
- Use authorized, documented procedures.

### Methods of Obtaining Evidence

| Method | Description |
|---|---|
| Consent | Voluntary permission to search or surrender evidence |
| Subpoena | Legal order compelling production of evidence |
| Search Warrant | Judicial authorization to search/seize within defined scope |
| Plain View | Qualifying evidence lawfully observed by law enforcement |
| Exigent Circumstances | Emergency conditions may justify warrantless action |

### Important
- Search warrants generally require probable cause and specific scope.
- Privacy and search requirements depend on jurisdiction.
- Workplace searches must comply with applicable privacy laws.

### Interview vs. Interrogation
- **Interview:** Gather information from a person.
- **Interrogation:** Question a suspected participant, potentially for use in criminal proceedings.

### Investigation Reporting
- Document investigation objectives, procedures, collected evidence, and findings.
- Preserve relevant records for possible legal proceedings.
- Consult management/legal counsel when involving law enforcement.
- Never conduct unauthorized counterattacks or hack back.

## 7. Computer Crime Categories

| Category | Primary Motivation |
|---|---|
| Military / Intelligence | Obtain classified or restricted intelligence |
| Business | Steal trade secrets or compromise business assets |
| Financial | Illegally obtain money or financial benefits |
| Terrorist | Cause fear and disrupt essential operations |
| Grudge | Revenge or retaliation |
| Thrill | Excitement, challenge, or recognition |
| Hacktivist | Political or ideological objectives |

### Additional Concepts
- **APT:** Sophisticated, persistent, well-resourced threat targeting specific objectives.
- **Corporate Espionage:** Unauthorized acquisition of competitive business information.
- **Insider Threat:** Malicious or accidental harm originating from trusted individuals.
- **Script Kiddie:** Uses existing attack tools with limited technical expertise.

## 8. ISC2 Code of Professional Ethics

### Four Canons — Memorize in Priority Order

1. **Protect society, the common good, necessary public trust and confidence, and the infrastructure.**

2. **Act honorably, honestly, justly, responsibly, and legally.**

3. **Provide diligent and competent service to principals.**

4. **Advance and protect the profession.**

### Important
- Canons are listed in priority order.
- Protection of society takes precedence over employer interests when duties conflict.
- Compliance with the ISC2 Code of Ethics is mandatory for certification.

### Ethics Complaints — Standing
- **Canons I and II:** Members of the public.
- **Canon III:** Employers or contractual principals.
- **Canon IV:** Other professionals.
- Violations may result in sanctions, including certification revocation.

## 9. Other Ethics Frameworks

### Organizational Code of Ethics
- Defines expected professional conduct.
- Covers integrity, confidentiality, conflicts of interest, and responsible behavior.

### RFC 1087 — Ethics and the Internet
Identifies the following as unethical:
- Unauthorized access.
- Disrupting intended Internet use.
- Wasting computing/network resources.
- Destroying information integrity.
- Compromising privacy.

### Code of Fair Information Practices
1. No secret personal-data recordkeeping systems.
2. Individuals can discover what data is held and how it is used.
3. Personal data should not be reused for unrelated purposes without consent.
4. Individuals can correct inaccurate information.
5. Organizations must ensure data reliability and prevent misuse.

### Ten Commandments of Computer Ethics
- Respect privacy, intellectual property, and authorized use.
- Do not use computers for theft, deception, harm, or unauthorized interference.
- Consider the social consequences of technology.
