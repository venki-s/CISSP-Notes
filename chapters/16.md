
# Chapter 16 — Managing Security Operations

**CISSP Domains:** 2, 3, 7, 8  
**Primary Domain:** 7 — Security Operations

---

## 1. Foundational Security Operations

### Due Care vs. Due Diligence
- **Due Care:** Taking reasonable measures to protect organizational assets.
- **Due Diligence:** Ongoing verification that security measures remain appropriate and effective.
- Senior management is ultimately responsible for both.

### Need to Know vs. Least Privilege
- **Need to Know:** Access only to information required for assigned duties.
- **Least Privilege:** Minimum permissions and system rights necessary to perform assigned duties.
- Applies to users, services, applications, and processes.

### Segregation of Duties (SoD)
- Divides critical functions among multiple individuals.
- Reduces fraud, unauthorized activities, and errors.
- Collusion can defeat SoD.

| Control | Distinction |
|---|---|
| SoD | Different people perform separate critical functions |
| Two-Person Control | Two authorized individuals required for one operation |
| Split Knowledge | Critical information divided so no individual possesses it all |
| Job Rotation | Employees exchange responsibilities; provides peer review and cross-training |
| Mandatory Vacation | Another employee temporarily takes over duties, helping expose concealed fraud |

- **Job Rotation / Mandatory Vacation:** Detective and deterrent controls against insider fraud.

## 2. Privileged Access Management (PAM)

- Restricts, monitors, and audits the use of privileged accounts.
- Applies to administrator, root, domain administrator, and other elevated accounts.

### Just-in-Time (JIT) Administration
- Grants elevated privileges only when required.
- Privileges are time-limited and expire automatically.
- Reduces the risk of standing administrative privileges.

### Privileged Activity Monitoring
- Monitors account creation, privilege changes, firewall modifications, and audit-log access.
- Helps detect insider abuse, privilege escalation, and advanced persistent threat (APT) activity.

**Exam:** PAM controls privileged access; JIT provides temporary elevation.

## 3. Service-Level Agreements

### SLA
- Formal agreement defining service performance expectations.
- May specify availability, response requirements, and penalties.

### MOU
- Documents an intention to cooperate.
- Generally less formal and does not normally contain financial penalties.

**Exam:** Enforceable vendor performance commitments → SLA.

## 4. Personnel Safety and Security

- **Personnel safety takes priority over protecting physical assets.**
- Emergency exits must support safe evacuation.
- **Duress Systems:** Silent alarms, panic buttons, duress codes, and verification phrases.
- **Travel Security:** Minimize sensitive data on devices, use encryption, secure connectivity/VPNs, and protect devices from theft or tampering.
- **Emergency Management:** Plans and training to protect people during disasters.
- **Security Awareness:** Insider threats, social media exposure, social engineering, and MFA/2FA fatigue.

**Exam:** When equipment security conflicts with life safety, prioritize personnel safety.

## 5. Asset and Media Management

### Asset Ownership
- **Asset Owner:** Senior manager ultimately accountable for protection requirements.
- **Data Custodian:** Implements day-to-day protection, storage, backups, and access controls.
- Responsibility remains with the owner even when tasks are delegated.

### Asset Inventory
- **Tangible Assets:** Physical equipment.
- **Software Assets:** Applications, operating systems, and licenses.
- **Intangible Assets:** Intellectual property, trademarks, patents, copyrights, and reputation.
- Asset inventories track ownership, location, status, and life-cycle changes.
- Barcodes and RFID can support physical inventory.

### Media Protection
- Protect storage media according to data classification.
- Apply appropriate physical security, access controls, and encryption.
- Removable media creates malware introduction and data-exfiltration risks.
- Secure backup media during storage and transportation.
- Maintain protected off-site backups for disaster recovery.

### Media Sanitization
- Sanitize media before reuse or disposal.
- Select sanitization methods based on media type and sensitivity.
- **Degaussing:** Applicable to magnetic media; ineffective for SSDs.
- **Physical Destruction:** Appropriate when secure sanitization cannot be assured.
- **NIST SP 800-88:** Media sanitization guidance.

### MTTF vs. MTBF
- **MTTF:** Mean Time to Failure; commonly used for nonrepairable items.
- **MTBF:** Mean Time Between Failures; commonly used for repairable systems.

## 6. Cloud Security Operations

### Shared Responsibility Model

| Responsibility | IaaS | PaaS | SaaS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Physical infrastructure | Provider | Provider | Provider |
| Operating system/runtime | Customer | Provider | Provider |
| Deployed application | Customer | Customer | Provider |
| Customer data and access controls | Customer | Customer | Customer |

- **IaaS:** Customer manages OS, applications, and data.
- **PaaS:** Customer manages deployed applications and data.
- **SaaS:** Provider manages the application; customer retains responsibility for its data, identities, and available security configurations.
- Customer accountability for data does not disappear when services are outsourced.

### Cloud Deployment Models
- **Public:** Shared cloud services offered to customers.
- **Private:** Dedicated to one organization; may be externally hosted.
- **Community:** Shared by organizations with common requirements.
- **Hybrid:** Combination of distinct cloud environments with integration/portability.

### Cloud Data Protection
- Encrypt sensitive data at rest and in transit.
- Define encryption-key ownership and management responsibilities.
- **Cryptographic Erase:** Destroys encryption keys so properly encrypted data becomes inaccessible.

### Managed Services
- **MSP:** Outsourced IT management.
- **MSSP:** Outsourced security management.
- **SECaaS:** Cloud-delivered security services.

### Scalability vs. Elasticity
- **Scalability:** Ability to handle increased demand by adding resources.
- **Elasticity:** Dynamically adds and removes resources according to demand.

### Serverless / FaaS
- Provider manages underlying execution infrastructure.
- Customer manages function/application code.
- Functions typically execute when invoked and scale independently.
- Serverless does not mean no servers exist.

## 7. Configuration Management (CM)

### Purpose
- Establishes and maintains secure, consistent system configurations throughout their life cycle.

### Provisioning and Hardening
- Configure systems securely before deployment.
- Disable unused services and ports.
- Remove unnecessary applications.
- Change default credentials.

### Security Baseline
- Approved starting configuration defining required security settings.
- Provides a reference for evaluating configuration changes.

### Baseline Images
- Preconfigured, tested images used for consistent deployment.
- Must be protected against unauthorized modification and malware.

### Configuration Automation
- Applies standard configurations consistently.
- Reduces manual errors and configuration drift.

### Configuration Documentation
- Records system ownership, purpose, current settings, and changes to the baseline.

**Exam:** Configuration Management maintains the approved secure state of systems.

## 8. Change Management

### Purpose
- Controls changes to reduce unauthorized modifications, security weaknesses, and unintended outages.
- Requires security-impact analysis before implementing changes.

### Change Management Process
1. **Request** the change.
2. **Review** operational and security impacts.
3. **Approve/Reject** through authorized personnel or a Change Advisory Board (CAB).
4. **Test** in a nonproduction environment when possible.
5. **Schedule and Implement** the approved change.
6. **Document** the change and update configuration records.

### Key Controls
- **CAB:** Reviews and approves/rejects proposed changes.
- **Rollback/Backout Plan:** Restores the previous state after an unsuccessful change.
- **Version Control:** Tracks software/configuration revisions.
- **Emergency Changes:** May follow expedited approval but still require documentation and appropriate review.

### CM vs. Change Management
- **Configuration Management:** Maintains and records approved configurations.
- **Change Management:** Controls how modifications are evaluated, authorized, tested, and implemented.

**Exam:** Prevent unplanned outages from system modifications → Change Management.

## 9. Patch Management

### Purpose
- Applies relevant software updates to correct security vulnerabilities and defects.
- Covers servers, workstations, network devices, mobile devices, and embedded/IoT systems.

### Patch Management Process
1. **Evaluate:** Determine applicability and urgency.
2. **Test:** Identify possible side effects.
3. **Approve:** Authorize deployment.
4. **Deploy:** Apply the patch.
5. **Verify:** Confirm successful installation and effectiveness.

### Important
- Test before widespread deployment when feasible.
- Critical vulnerabilities may require expedited patching.
- Patches can introduce availability or compatibility problems.
- Patch deployment must be verified, not merely assumed successful.

## 10. Vulnerability Management

- Continuously identifies, evaluates, prioritizes, and mitigates weaknesses.
- Uses vulnerability scans and periodic assessments.
- Coordinates with patch management.

### Patch vs. Vulnerability Management
- **Patch Management:** Installs fixes.
- **Vulnerability Management:** Identifies and addresses vulnerabilities, including those that cannot be patched.

### Risk Treatment
- Remediation may involve patches, configuration changes, or compensating controls.
- **Residual Risk:** Risk remaining after controls are applied.
- Management may formally accept residual risk when remediation is not appropriate or feasible.
- Risk acceptance must be authorized and documented.

### Segregation of Duties
- One team may implement patches.
- Another team independently verifies patch status.

### CVE
- **Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures.**
- Standardized identifiers for publicly known vulnerabilities.
- Used by vulnerability scanning and patch management tools.

**Exam:** Identifying vulnerabilities is not sufficient; findings require risk-based treatment and verification.
